Nokka Joinery

Furniture built to outlive the room.

In Gothenburg’s industrial fringe, a small group of craftsmen are challenging the throwaway culture of modern home design. Their manifesto is simple: build things that can be repaired, not replaced.

Figure 04: Kessel Lounge Chair (Schematics)

Every piece that exits the workshop carries the mark of the hands that built it. We do not use veneered particleboard or plastic dowels. Instead, we source full logs of Swedish ash and American black walnut, carving them into raw billets.

The joinery is a study in architectural history. Double-mortise and tenon wood joints are secured by small unlacquered brass locking pins. Over fifty years, the wood moves with the seasons—breathing in the winter dry air and expanding under summer humidity.

Veneer furniture splits and peels; mass-manufactured plastics crack. But solid timber, rubbed with matte cold-pressed linseed oil and beeswax compound, wears the way skin tans. If damaged, it can be planed down and refinished. It is built to outlive the room, the house, and the century.
